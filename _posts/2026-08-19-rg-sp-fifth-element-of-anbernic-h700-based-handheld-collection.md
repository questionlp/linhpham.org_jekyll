A year ago, I wrote about the [four Anbernic handhelds built around an Allwinner H700]({{ site_url }}{% post_url 2025-08-13-tale-of-four-anbernic-h700-based-handhelds%}) system on a chip I had purchased over a period of time. Two of those were heavily-inspired by two of my favorite Nintendo handheld consoles: the original Nintendo Game Boy Advance (RG34XX H) and the Game Boy Advance SP (RG35XX SP). Even with the firm and clacky buttons, I brought the RG35XX SP with me on trips due to the flip format factor protecting the screen when the device was not in use.

When I heard that Anbernic was going to announce (yet) another handheld based around the Allwinner H700 and heavily inspired by the Game Boy Advance SP, I kind of rolled my eyes. The RG34XX SP that was released after the RG35XX SP was a huge miss for me for a number of reasons. So, when I saw that the new handheld, the [RG SP (or RGSP)](https://anbernic.com/products/rg-sp), was a slimmer version of the RG35XX SP and had a screen with a usable 3:2 aspect ratio, I ended up placing an order for one in Pearl Pink the moment orders opened up for 57.99 USD after early bird discount and before shipping. The version I ordered only came with a 16 GB TF/microSD card with the stock Anbernic firmware.

Why Pearl Pink? I didn't want one in Platinum (silver) or Graphite (dark gray), and I have been on a bit of a lavender, pink, purple, and violet kick lately.

### Why Another One?

Even though I had been spending some of my time between various handheld consoles, I still wanted something that was a lot closer to the Game Boy Advance SP (AGS-101) that I absolutely love. While the ergonomics isn't great, it was the perfect form factor for me but I definitely needed something with a slightly larger screen.

My hope was that the RG35XX SP could fill that need, but it's a really compromised device. Between the larger size compared to the GBA SP (especially the thickness), the aforementioned firm and clacky buttons (even after doing a tape mod), and the 3:4 aspect ratio screen all added up to the handheld falling quite short of being *the* modern GBA SP for me.

Over time, I ended up either bringing the RG35XX H or the RG40XX V along with me, depending on which bag I was toting around with me. So, I hoped that the RG SP would be that modern GBA SP.

### Initial Impressions

When the RG SP arrived, I had my GBA SP and RG35XX SP on hand to compare the size, ergonomics, and the overall fit and finish. Upon removing the RG SP from the box, I was pretty excited to see how close it was to the GBA SP in terms of size and weight.

I flipped open the RG SP and was met with a face button layout that matched the RG35XX SP, though the dish areas around the buttons and D-pad were closer in depth to the GBA SP than the Chicago deep-dish area on the RG35XX SP. This means that the buttons and D-pad stick out a lot more compared to the deck of the handheld than both the GBA SP and the RG35XX SP.

While the handheld was still powered off and the TF/microSD card with the stock firmware removed, I played around with and compared the face and should buttons, and the D-pad, on all three handhelds. While the face buttons and D-pad on the RG SP aren't as firm or as clacky as my pre-modded RG35XX SP, they were still firmer than on my GBA SP with its original button membranes and switches.

Since the face buttons and the D-pad stick out more compared to the GBA SP, I couldn't just glide my fingers across the deck like I could with the GBA SP or the RG35XX SP even with its deeper and larger dishes. Although not to my liking, they aren't deal breakers either. I'll just have to adjust how I move my thumb across the deck of the handheld while playing games.

Slightly less ideal for the RG SP were the L1 and R1 shoulder buttons. The same L1 and R1 buttons on the RG35XX SP and the L and R buttons on the GBA SP stand slightly proud from the back of the body and have very similar heights. This allows me to quickly feel where they are and press them with ease. On the RG SP, the shoulder buttons are flush to the body and are shorter in height. The latter is due to the reduced thickness of the bottom portion of the handheld. The size and positioning of the L1 and R2 buttons means that I have to spend just a little bit more time to feel where they are and, depending on my grip, the body of the handheld digs slightly into my finger when pressing the buttons.

On the positive side, I'm glad that the headphone jack is horizontally centered on the front of the handheld compared to the jack being to the left of the two TF/microSD card slots on the front of the RG35XX SP. Don't get me started on the #DongleLife that is the headphone situation for the GBA SP.

One thing I noticed when I went to charge the handheld and after powering it on was that the green and orange status LEDs are much dimmer than on the RG35XX SP and my GBA SP. At first, I thought the handheld wasn't charging or powering on until I looked at the side of the RG SP.

### KNULLI and Emulation Experience

When I removed the TF/microSD card with the stock firmware, I inserted the card into my multi-card reader with the write protect switch to set enabled and imaged the contents of the card. Once that was done, I wrote "RG SP" on the label and tucked it away. I pulled out a spare 16 GB microSD card from my stash and flashed it with a special build of [KNULLI](https://knulli.org/) for the RG34XX SP from their Discord server, since there isn't an official release for the RG SP yet.

I booted the RG SP up with the special build of KNULLI and started setting things up like I've done with the other H700-based handhelds that I have. For the second microSD card, I copied over the required files from the second microSD card in my RG34XX H, and inserted it into the RG SP.

Unlike the selection of games I have on my RG35XX SP and RG40XX V, I chose to limit the games I kept on RG34XX SP and RG SP to the Game Boy, Game Boy Color, Game Boy Advance, Game Gear, and TurboGrafx-16 consoles, along with the PICO-8 virtual console. Games from the Game Boy family are a natural fit since the GBA and GBA SP can also play Game Boy and Game Boy Color games. Games for the Game Gear handheld console makes sense for a handheld console. I included TurboGrafx-16 games since I mostly played them on a TurboExpress rather than on the home console.

After getting the RG SP connected to my wireless network, I set up Syncthing to sync the `/userdata/saves` directory between the other Anbernic H700-based devices via a Syncthing service running on a Raspberry Pi. After pairing up the RG SP with Syncthing on the Pi, I noticed that it wouldn't completely sync changes consistently with both ends reporting a status of 90+ percent syncing with zero bytes remaining. This does not happen with the other handhelds.

I am chalking this up to running a special build of KNULLI that can run on the RG SP. I hope this can get resolved once an official build is made for the next release of KNULLI.

Since the RG SP has the same Allwinner H700 system on a chip as my other Anbernic handhelds, it can easily handle any game from the initially limited selection of consoles even when using one of the sharp, bilinear shaders, rewind enabled and run-ahead latency reduction set to `Auto`.

The audio coming out of the single speaker a bit of a mixed bag, maybe being just slightly less clear than on the RG35XX SP. Compared to the original speaker in the GBA SP, it isn't quite as harsh of highs (without any audio filtering enabled) but lacks some of the definition in the mids.

Even though the screen is smaller than the RG35XX SP's screen by 0.1 inch (2.54 mm), I can make full use of the usable screen area while running Game Boy Advance games with integer scaling. There are a couple of times where not using integer scaling and using a sharp, bilinear shaders leads to some odd scaling artifacts on the RG35XX SP. The 3:2 screen aspect ratio designed for Game Boy and Game Boy Color games, but the original GBA and GBA SP were less optimal due to their smaller screens.

When playing Game Boy Advance games, the bottom of the RG SP gets warm. Unfortunately, due to the slimmer design of the RG SP, there is no door for the battery and reports are that there isn't a lot of clearance between the system on the chip and the battery on the bottom. It will be interesting to see how it fares long-term.

### Testing the Controls on the RG SP

To test how the D-pads perform on the RG SP versus the GBA SP, I tested the NES Classic version of Super Mario Bros. and Super Street Fighter II Turbo Revival, as both games would test how responsive or twitchy the D-pads are. In order for handhelds to score well, I'm looking for success rate of at least 75 percent when running, then duck sliding under the one-tile gap in World 1-2 as Super Mario. For Turbo Revival, I was looking for a 60 percent success rate of alternating between Hadokens and Shoryukens as Ryu facing left and right. I do not count Ryu's Shakunetsu Hadokens since that would mean an incorrect D-pad input.

With the GBA SP with its original button membranes, it just passes the Mario duck sliding test, but fails at the Turbo Revival test. For the latter, I could not consistently get Hadokens to register correctly but Shoryukens were perfectly fine.

For the RG35XX SP, it also passes the Mario duck sliding test with flying colors, but just barely passes the Turbo Revival test. Even though it does pass both tests, the firmness of the buttons made my thumbs sore after repeatedly trying both duck sliding and alternating between Hadokens and Shoryukens.

On the RG SP, it didn't pass the Mario duck sliding test as it doesn't always register a quick transition from a right press to a down press on the D-pad (even though I can hear the button clicks). This was also evident when trying to enter into the bonus room pipes in both World 1-1 and World 1-2. For the Turbo Revival test, it didn't pass the test and performed somewhere between the RG35XX SP and the GBA SP.

For comparison, the RG35XX H easily passes both tests. For the RG40XX V and one of my modded GBA, both pass the duck sliding test, while only the modded GBA passed the Turbo Revival test.

Maybe the issues could be partially attributed to the special build of KNULLI and could be resolved in the next official release. It may also require some use to break in the button membranes to improve the feel of the buttons and D-pad.

Outside of that, I haven't noticed any significant issues with the face buttons and D-pad while playing games like Tetris and Sword of Mana. I haven't fully gotten used to the flush-mounted L1 and R2 shoulder buttons, as they are not as ergonomic as shoulder buttons on the RG35XX SP or the GBA SP.

### Verdict So Far

Even with a couple of rough edges with the special build of KNULLI running on the RG SP and still needing to break in the button membranes, it has done enough to supplant the RG35XX SP as my preferred compact handheld console. The nearly idential dimensions to the GBA SP but with a larger screen is a huge plus for me. Hopefully an official build for the next version of KNULLI is coming soon for the RG SP to smooth over the current rough edges.

I will still choose the RG35XX H when I want to play games for the other 8-bit and 16-bit consoles due to its screen with a 4:3 aspect ratio, along with its better overall ergonomics. For newer handheld and home consoles, I have been carrying around the [AYN Thor](https://www.ayntec.com/products/ayn-thor) (in a Super Famicom-inspired Rainbow colorway) with its dual screens (perfect for Nintendo DS and 3DS games). The Thor is just too big and heavy to carry around everywhere and its power-hungry system on a chip and display(s) leads to its worse battery life and need for active cooling.

Although I have a [Retroid Pocket Nova](https://www.goretroid.com/collections/frontpage/products/retroid-pocket-nova-handheld) (in a Nintendo GameCube-inspired "Indigo" purple colorway) arriving in the coming weeks, it does not compete in any way with any of Anbernic handhelds that I have. It comes in at a wholly different level when it comes to its display, performance, and most likely, build quality. It has the same level as the AYN Odin 2 (in translucent purple) and an [AYN Odin 2 Portal](https://www.ayntec.com/products/odin2-portal) (in an indigo blue colorway) handhelds, but with a 4:3 aspect ratio instead of the AYN's 16:9 aspect ratio screen. That should make it a perfect handheld for playing games from the relatively newer generation of consoles (for example: the Nintendo GameCube, Sony PlayStation 2, and Sega Dreamcast).
